## A Gray Day in the East: Sands, Saxons and Sunshine

### Walberswick

As an initial disclaimer - this trip predates the "quest" part of this journal. I have made various
other jaunts out prior to 2026 when I decided to try and see as manu of the UK butterfly species as I could
on a journey to see all resident species. This trip takes us back to 2025, when I had taken the 
day off work on this Friday to plan a trip over to the east coast. 

The reason for this trip was really because I needed to collect some equipment from some 
colleagues over near to Orford - myself and collagues had been busy tracking the
movements of lesser black-backed gulls and herring gulls (as part of my day job) from a colony 
at Havergate island in the years previous and this kit had sat in an office over there 
for a good long while. However, with my partner along for this trip, we decided to combine 
this into a bit of a coastal jolly. 

We decided that one place we always wanted to visit, but hadn't was Sutton Hoo. 
Sutton Hoo is a historic Anglo-Saxon burial site near Woodbridge in Suffolk, famous for its early 
7th-century ship burial, believed to have been that of an important East Anglian king. 
Set in rolling countryside overlooking the River Deben, its ancient burial mounds, woodland 
and open grassland make it a fascinating blend of archaeological heritage and natural beauty.

However, another location had also piqued my interest - Walberswick. A little further over, just up the
coast, there had been a rather exciting sighting of rare bird - a zitting cisticola! There were even
reports of possible breeding evidence of them, and the timing was perfect to potentially hop over and also see them.
Suddenly, this had turned into a triple-threat of a trip! Regarding butterflies, this was not 
fully on my mind at the start I confess, but naturally, we could expect to see some coastal species. 
I went with just a good walk in mind, with anything that turns up being be a bonus.

We set off at a reasonable hour around 09:00 to make the first stop at Orford and collect the kit.
This was after taking some early morning pictures of goldfinches on teasels out the 
front of our house.

The clouds were heavy in the morning, gray skies. Navigating our way through the Cambridgeshire
and Suffolk countryside we made our way to Orford, off the beaten track a little, and
we successfully picked up the kit - and also delivered a box
of chocolates to say thanks to my colleague. They had been stars for the work they put in
over there so we exchanged pleasantries and reminisced a bit about the gulls, and parted ways. 

Then, we headed to part two of the trip - Walberswick. The sighting for the zitting cisticola had been
from a path heading south from the Cliff Field car park. The coastal 
path winds past extensive reedbeds and marshland alongside the Dunwich River, 
a rich wetland habitat attracting a variety of birdlife - this is perfect for the cisticolas. We
pulled up and had a very quick look around Walbersick.

some amazing views.

It was about midday by now. I recall my walking shoes falling a part - the soles had invited 
a bevvy of small stones into the shoes toe caps making life somewhat difficult, and a 
stark reminder that I needed a new pair. 

However, something caught my eye quite soon into the walk - a small brown butterfly
fluttering low on the shingle. It was a grayling!!

<figure class="report-figure">

This was amazing - it posed quite nicely and then proceeded to hunker down and hide its 'eye'
away, but I got quite close, before I spooked it. It fluttered along with us for a little bit
but then went its merry way. That was a great start!

We had also passed some folks coming back the other way and it wasn't too long until we
saw up ahead some people gaxing back into the marshes. This was where the sightings of the
zisticola were likely, as the spot that I had researched beforehand matched this exact description.
We cautiously approached the small group, of about six or seven people, and joined the party. 

We stood for probably 20 minutes or so, but in that time, we did indeed see one! It was very
distant, and we clearly heard the "zit" call over the fairly brisk coastal breeze. So the views
were not great, but I certainly caught sight of it. No picure I am afraid, but with the one below
it was probably in there somewhere 😊.

After that we headed back along the same path to the Cliff Field car park, and tucked into a 
bit of much needed lunch.

It was then time for part three of the day heading off to Sutton Hoo.

### Sutton Hoo

We were excited to visit Sutton Hoo. The site contains one of Britain's most significant 
archaeological discoveries, including the famous ship burial and the remarkable collection 
of gold and garnet treasures. The site is managed by the National Trust and we made the
short trip over from Walberswick and pulled up into the car park with ease. The skies 
had brightened a bit too by this point. 

We headed in, and had a quick look around the museum close to the entrance that houses some
fascinating objects and reconstructions that bring the Anglo-Saxon world to life. We also made out
way by the reconstruction of the 27-metre-long ship discovered beneath Mound 1, that was impressive.

Many of the famous treasures from the original ship burial are now at the British Museum in London. 
Sutton Hoo has replicas of those treasures, alongside some genuine archaeological finds from 
other excavations at the site. 

After a quick Cornetto (!), we headed out on the trails to the ancient burial mounds.

<figure class="report-figure">

These mounds rise gently from the surrounding grassland - they are remarkable earthworks that mark the 
resting places of some of the most important figures in early Anglo-Saxon East Anglia.
We also scaled a tower overlooking the beautiful Deben valley.

We then made our way back on a looping trail in the direction of the visitor centre, which
took us through some great woodland and bracken. 

<figure class="report-figure">

Here we saw various butterflies such as speckled 
wood, meadow brown and red admiral. However, we also spotted another grayling perched on the side
of a tree! (Not an actual "tree grayling", which is a different species not native to the UK 😁)

<figure class="report-figure">

It was a bout 15:15 by now and other birds had showed well along our route back
such as migrants such as whitethroats, and other species such as buzzards, red kites and 
in the coniferous areas, coal tits and gold crests. We stopped 
for refreshments at the local cafe and sat on some outside benches. The cafe here was really 
quite nice here and could have stayed much longer - we will have to come back. 
We had a quick browse in the visitor centre too before we left. 

This had been a great day. We had packed quite a lot in and quite tired by now, we made our way back
with a big highlight for me seeing the graylings. Although the skies had started grey, they certainly
brightened with the grayling!
