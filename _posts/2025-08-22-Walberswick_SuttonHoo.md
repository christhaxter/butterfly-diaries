### Walberswick

As an initial disclaimer - this trip predates the "quest" part of this journal. I have made various
other jaunts out prior to 2026, before I got hooked with the idea to try and see as many of the UK 
butterfly species as I could. This trip therefore takes us back to 2025, on a Friday in August, when I had 
taken the day off work for a trip over to the east coast. 

Not that it is of interest, but the reason for this excursion was because I needed 
to collect some equipment from some colleagues over near to Orford. As part of my day job, we had been busy 
tracking the movements of lesser black-backed gulls and herring gulls from a breeding colony of gulls
at Havergate island in the years previous, and this kit had sat in an office over there 
for a good long while. However, with my partner along for this trip, we decided to combine 
it into a bit of a coastal jolly. 

One place we had always wanted to visit, but as yet had failed to see, was Sutton Hoo. 
Sutton Hoo is a historic Anglo-Saxon burial site near Woodbridge in Suffolk, famous for its early 
7th-century ship burial, believed to have been that of an important East Anglian king. 
Set in rolling countryside overlooking the River Deben, its ancient burial mounds, woodland 
and open grassland make it a fascinating blend of archaeological heritage and natural beauty.

Another location had also piqued my interest over that way - Walberswick. This was just a bit further up the coast
from Orford, and here, there had been a rather exciting sighting of rare bird - a zitting cisticola! 
There was even talk of possible breeding evidence, and the timing was perfect 
to potentially hop over and also see them. Suddenly, this had turned into a triple-threat of a trip, Orford to
Walberswick to Sutton Hoo! Regarding butterflies, this was not fully on my mind at the start I confess, 
but we could expect to see some coastal species. I went with just a good walk in mind, with anything that 
turns up being be a bonus.

We set off at a reasonable hour around 09:00 to make the first stop at Orford and collect the kit.
This was after taking some early morning pictures of goldfinches on teasels out the 
front of our house.

The clouds were heavy in the morning, grey skies. Navigating our way through the Cambridgeshire
and Suffolk countryside we made our way to Orford/ This was off the beaten track a little to reach the offices
of our colleagues, but we successfully picked up the kit - and also delivered a box
of chocolates to say thanks for all their hard work. We exchanged pleasantries and reminisced a bit about 
the gulls, and parted ways. 

Then, we headed to part two of the trip - Walberswick. The sighting for the zitting cisticola had been
from a path heading south-west from the Cliff Field car park. The coastal 
path runs past fairly extensive reedbeds and marshland alongside the Dunwich River, 
and is a rich wetland habitat attracting a variety of birdlife - this sounded perfect for the cisticolas. We
pulled up and had a very quick look around Walbersick.

with a lovely summer coastal vibe. Before we knew it, we were up on the shingle path right by the seafront, with
some amazing views.

It was about midday by now. I recall my walking shoes falling apart on this trip - the soles had invited 
a bevvy of small stones into the shoe toe caps, making life somewhat difficult, and a 
stark reminder that I needed a new pair. 

However, something distracted me quite soon into the walk - a small brown butterfly
fluttering low on the shingle. It was a grayling!

<figure class="report-figure">

This was amazing - it posed quite nicely and then proceeded to hunker down and hide its 'eye'
away among the stones on the ground. I got quite close, before I spooked it. It fluttered along with us 
for a little while but then went on its merry way. That was a great start though!

We had also passed some folks coming back the other way on the path, and just a bit further along we
spied some other people a short distance ahead gazing back into the marsh within scopes and binoculars. 
This was where the sightings of the zisticola were likely to be, as the spot that I had researched 
beforehand matched this exact description. We cautiously approached the small group, 
of about six or seven people, and joined the party. 

We stood for probably 20-30 minutes, but in that time, we did indeed see one! It was very
distant, but we clearly heard the "zit-zit" call over the fairly brisk coastal breeze. I certainly caught sight 
of it flitting among the reeds, however with no picture souvenir. It is probably in the picture
below somewhere 😊.

A wall brown had also breezed by us too, which I also failed to get a picture of. That was cool though, and
the coastal butterflies were certainly delivering today. After that we headed back along the same path back to the 
Cliff Field car park, and tucked into a bit of much needed lunch. That had been a very successful start to the day.

However, it was then time for part three.

### Sutton Hoo

We were very excited to visit Sutton Hoo. The site contains one of Britain's most significant 
archaeological discoveries, including the famous ship burial and the remarkable collection 
of gold and garnet treasures. The site is managed by the National Trust and we made the
short trip over from Walberswick and pulled up into the car park with ease. The skies 
had brightened a bit too by this point. 

We headed in, and had a quick look around the museum close to the entrance that houses some
fascinating objects and reconstructions that bring the Anglo-Saxon world to life. We also made out
way by the reconstruction of the 27-metre-long ship discovered beneath Mound 1, that was impressive.

Many of the famous treasures from the original ship burial are now at the British Museum in London. 
Sutton Hoo has replicas of those treasures, alongside some genuine archaeological finds from 
other excavations at the site. 

After a quick Cornetto (!), we headed out on the trails to the ancient burial mounds.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Walberswick_SuttonHoo/Mound_route.jpg"

These mounds rise gently from the surrounding grassland - they are remarkable earthworks that mark the 
resting places of some of the most important figures in early Anglo-Saxon East Anglia.
We also scaled a tower overlooking the beautiful Deben valley.

We then made our way back on a looping trail in the direction of the visitor centre, which
took us through some great woodlands with plenty of bracken. 

<figure class="report-figure">

Here we saw various butterflies such as speckled 
wood, meadow brown and red admiral. However, we also spotted another grayling perched on the side
of a tree! (Not an actual "tree grayling", which is a different species not native to the UK 😁)

It was about 15:15 by now and other birds had showed well along our route back
such as migrants such as whitethroats, and other species such as buzzards, red kites and 
in the coniferous areas, coal tits and goldcrests. We stopped 
for refreshments at the local cafe and sat on the outside benches. The cafe here was really 
quite nice here and could have stayed much longer - we will have to come back. 
We had a quick browse in the visitor centre too before we left. 

We had packed quite a lot in so far, and we were quite tired by this point. Therefore, we made our way back home.
That evening, we also decided to watch the film about Sutton Hoo on Netflix, which fully rounded things off.
This had been a successful trip. Although the skies had started grey, they certainly brightened with the grayling!
