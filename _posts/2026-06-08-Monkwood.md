## Wood white wonder at Monkwood
### Monkwood, Worcestershire

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Monkwood/Wood_white_3.JPG"
       alt="Wood white">
</figure>

A planned visit across to see our nephew and nieces, in Tewksbury prompted a thought to 
chance a visit to a site nearby that was well timed for wood white, the site: Monkwood, 
Worcestershire, famously good for this species. I think I had seen them before in my youth 
in other areas, but I was treating this as an lifer tick.

Departing on a Friday taking annual leave heading west, my partner and I had hopes to swing 
by the site on the way to our relatives. But as with the whole 2026 season so far, the weather 
has never quite been on our side. This time was no different as arriving in the Worcestershire 
area, the rain, oh the rain, how it completely mismatched the forecast. A series of low pressure 
systems had been sweeping over the UK after the last heatwave broke, that completely contrasted the 30 degree conditons that preceded it. I went to the woodland anyway, knowing the forecast was not great, stoic.

Monkwood is managed by the Wildlife Trusts and Butterfly Conservation, and it was easy to find, 
and has a large circular car park. It must have looked quite bizarre (if anyone was watching) – a 
random person just stood standing in a woodland in the rain. But actually, this did stand us 
in good stead as it gave me some very useful intel about the layout of the site. At other 
times of the year the site hosts many other species like white admirals but wood whites are 
heavily featured on noticeboards.

  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Monkwood/Sign_1.jpg"

I left the woodland, not sure if that was the last we would see of it, as the forecast was 
still very mixed for the coming days, and it was all dependent on time available on the way 
home.

However, after a lovely weekend with the relatives, on the Monday return leg, we had a bit 
more luck with the weather. We spent the early part of the morning pottering around 
Tewkesbury retail park until around 10 am, at which time I knew the rain band we currently 
had was moving over to give some sunny weather behind it, with temperatures pushing up 
into the high teens, maybe higher. After gathering a stash of chocolates from a fantastic 
lindor shop, we then decided to have a quick coffee and then on the way home travelling 
East, swing back by Monkwood.

Cloud watching from the car, by the time we got to the site, the clouds had now parted, 
rain stopped and the woodland had spring to life. We had got the timing right. This was 
around lunchtime, so I jumped out of the car after parking up, and scouted out the same 
areas I saw on Friday. From the rain-soaked reccy of the previous Friday, I had spotted 
a separate butterfly walkway entrance across the road, with a metal archway inviting you 
in. I tried that first. On going through, and onto the soggy path freshly-soaked from the 
rain, it was buzzing with large Skippers, speckled woods, commas and more.

Wet underfoot, wellies were needed. No wood whites yet.

Then I went back to the exact main entrance from the circular car park where I had 
stood in the rain and immediately a wood white fluttered by! Bingo. A quick photo 
took the pressure off, as the mission was accomplished. Now I could get on with the 
business of just enjoying them.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Monkwood/Wood_white_1.jpg"

After some sandwiches, we both headed back across the road to the butterfly area, 
but again we saw no wood whites again on those trails, not venturing that far due to time. 
However a spotted flycatcher was great to hear. A great info board was place there though 
with information on species management.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Monkwood/Sign_2.jpg"
       alt="Noticeboard detailing woodland management at Monkwood">
  <figcaption>Noticeboard detailing woodland management at Monkwood</figcaption>

We then ventured back to the trusty main entrance route from the circular car park 
where I had just seen a wood white, and, wow, there they were! At least six to eight 
of them in total, with great views in the straight ride that led deeper into the wood.

The wood really was alive now in the humid post rain sunny glades. These were wide, 
open woodland rides and glades with some areas caged off probably to encourage flowers 
and prevent animal browsing.

<figure class="report-figure">

We had very little time to explore any more. Large skippers were also stealing the show, 
as well as common blue and an amazing support cast to the wood white leading roles.

It was quite magical in the end, and I was very reluctant to leave the wood whites. 
That was species 36. I didn’t stop enough to document the walkways and habitat in as 
much detail as this really was just a fleeting visit. The path we were on invited you 
into the woodland, and who knows, maybe we will return in another year.

It was also a lesson that persistence and weather watching was the name of the game to 
beat the low pressure systems that sought to throw curve balls at our plans. We then made 
the drive back east, and onto the next plan and trip.
