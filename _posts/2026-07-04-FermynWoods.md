## A royal persuit at Fermyn Woods (part 2)
### Fermyn Woods, Northamptonshire

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Fermyn_Woods/2026_07_04/Purple_emperor_best.JPG"
       alt="Purple emperor">
</figure>

The first visit to Fermyn had been a bit of a grind but an triumph overall in finding the black 
hairstreak. However, the idea was always to return, knowing the amazing variety of woodland 
specialists and grassland natives deserved further attention. For instance, there are dingy 
and grizzled skippers here earlier in the year. But there was only one species on my mind at 
this point in the season, their majesty, the purple emperor.

The website information had suggested walking a set route through Fermyn, and in particular on 
the last visit, I had found the first crossroad junction already, and a glade on the map that 
looked tempting. The author of the website report noted a first sighting of theirs at that 
location years earlier. However, there are many other woodland sections to this site, with 
woodlands also further to the south at Lady and Souther Woods being well worth a look. With 
that in mind, I thought it would be better to park closer to the place where the emperors may 
be, to save a walk from the main country park car park. We decided therefore to head to the 
top of the same glade that joined back up with where I originally walked to. This was a simple 
pull off the road spot where we could just walk down to the previously identified glade.

The weather again was up to its old tricks. It was the wind this time and an overall cloudier 
affair than during the week. We targeted the Saturday. Temperatures were fine, the wind was 
scheduled to pick up after lunch, with gusts up to 28 mph, average at 15 to 16 mph. Would 
that affect emperors coming down? I had done a lot of thinking about the wind direction, 
being westerly, and whether the woodland would take the sting out of the wind strength, or 
create turbulence, whip through rides or just make no difference, hopefully the latter.

The advice is to get out early for emperors as they can come down to the ground from their 
canopy spots and master trees. That is what we decided to do, and we headed out early, 
arriving at Fermyn at around 8:45 am. The rough plan was to hit that first main glade up 
from that crossroads path, and then make our way around other large rides and meander over 
to the Lady and Souther Wood complex. We drove past the turning for Fermyn country park and 
took the first right to bring us around and directly to the main woodland spot. Parked up, 
shoes on, the sun was out, coffee in hand, we were ready.

We headed down the bridleway. Walking was easy at first, but further down the path became 
more churned up, from horses probably, which I thought could be good for emperors. Butterflies 
were everywhere, lots of red admirals, gatekeepers, meadow browns, speckled woods, large skippers 
too. This was initially more meadow type habitat nearer the car that formed the wide glades but 
gradually transitioned to deeper wood flora and fauna. The ground was quite dry. I was a bit 
worried about emperors not coming down if there was no moisture, but salts, minerals, maybe it 
will be ok. When we got to the aforementioned clearing, it was a bit breezy, but not too bad. 
We stopped for quite a bit, 15 mins maybe, with no sign of the star of the show. Various browns 
took centre stage among the oaks and understory.

A gap in the vegetation looked inviting from the clearing, which was actually a small path 
leading totally the wrong direction. But after an initial peer through, I almost immediately 
spotted a purple….hairstreak. it was nectaring on I think hedge bedstraw. It wouldn’t fully 
cooperate for a photo but that was a cool find.

This was a nice sunny spot. It felt very pleasant, as everything was warming up, but we 
retreated back to the clearing. After deciding nothing else was happening there, we headed on.

The main bridleway was very much more open and wider than I had expected. But the flowers, 
grasses and general butterfly community was still strong all the way along.

After seeing another couple of purple hairstreaks fly by, we eventually came to the same 
crossroads I had walked to from my first visit, feeling a nice sense of familiarity. Turning 
left here from our direction took us further into Fermyn.

This next ride was much more grassy, and tighter, but still open and sunny with lots of 
meadowsweet. My partner was really on it today. I was a bit more eager to stomp on but 
calling me back, she found a purple hairstreak and it posed so well on a rye grass stem. 
Although upside down, so I reversed the image. This was the best hairstreak shot of the day.

I hadn’t expected emperors at this grassy bit, and I was making decisions of where to go on the 
fly, with the structure never being the same in reality as from the 2D overhead map that I 
had burnt into my retinas. Pressing on, we hit a T-junction, then hung a right, and darted 
off left down a smaller wooded path that was delightfully quaint. We dordled a bit, as where 
the path widened, it looked inviting. Oaks were abundant in this section

There were plenty of gatekeepers and other browns here. As we moved on and after a bit of 
route confusion, we eventually came out onto a large access road that we were intended to 
follow, as it was the best way to get around to Lady and Souther Woods. It was quite warm 
by now too.

On this path we stopped a couple of times at tempting looking sections and clearings. It 
wasn’t all solid oaks in this part, some was coniferous. But what caught my attention was 
a massive blue emperor dragonfly flying around that then perched obligingly.

So we had seen purple hairstreak, and an emperor dragonfly, we just needed to put the words 
together!
  
There were various log piles along this access road that we stopped at. This was after 
hearing some reports of purple emperors being around some burnt logs, but we had no idea 
where that was. These were just piles of timber. The path then led around out of Fermyn 
and we then found ourselves out of the wood and in a field landscape; a nice vista. We 
followed the path, heading across the field as the next wood came closer into view. So 
far Fermyn Woods had not delivered the main prize but there was still time, nearing 9:45 
at this point, I had heard good things too about the next wood.

The entrance to this second wood is apparently good for white hairstreak with elms nearby. 
There was some bramble here and flowers. I stopped a bit here scanning the tree tops and flowers, 
but the westerly wind was wipping through, this being a west-side location, and the sun was 
leaving us for a bit at this point, so this wasn’t so fruitful.

Out of the wind right at the neck of the woodland here though, things improved. Across the 
other side of the path, there was a flash of orange, it was a silver-washed fritillary!

A couple of them were dancing around the brambles so this was excellent, and quite a fresh 
looking one too.

We treked on a bit further, but I had moved off too quick. My other half stopped me and 
exclaimed that she had seen a dark brown butterfly dart up from some dung in front of us. 
There was a bit of animal dung on the paths in this section. We had also come equipped with 
some unusual props – salmon fish paste! Emperors are drawn to rotting scents, dung, fruits 
etc. But I had read that fish paste was an idea to bait with. That came into play here as 
we decided to start leaving some, although it takes a while to get the scent in the air and 
with the wind, and we had no idea if this was bonkers.

I had seen the briefest of a dash of something when my partner called out, but I was cursing 
myself for storming off too fast and the fact we might have just missed an emperor. We decided 
to stop longer to wait to see if it came back, still near the fritillaries. She described it 
as a large brown butterfly that flew differently and it sounded very much like an emperor. We 
waited. We baited. Ten minutes later, we decided to move on after water and suncream top ups, 
and planned to come back here later if time, maybe once the paste had worked its magic. With 
wildlife spotting, I find it so easy to fall into the trap where you become convinced you are 
not going to see anything. That can influence how you search. It requires patience and belief, 
and this potential encounter had helped me keep the mindset that anything is possible.

A bit further on, the path came to a fork, and here there was a bubbling muddy patch off to the 
side, which I started convincing myself would be attractive to something for moisture. There 
were a lot of flies around it. I baited more salmon paste near here too. To be honest this 
baiting idea was starting to feel foolhardy. Nothing here though.

We headed right at the fork and this section looked good – the oaks were a bit further back 
from the wide access road. The route had not transitioned into any smaller pathways and there 
was some grassy scrubby understory bordering the path. There was a fair bit of sunlight peaking 
through here too as the clouds had parted a bit.

More dung (!) was here on the path and then I startled, as I saw two butterflies on it from 
distance.

then met two guys and we asked them if they had seen any emperors. To which they replied, “oh

direction we were heading. They showed a cracking video one had taken of a male. They had said 
there were males and females, and in fact they came earlier today having been too late the day

they were here, and at the right time of day. I was trying not to feel incredibly envious of 
what they had filmed. But this was great news and I was pleased for them too. Again everyone 
here was friendly. We were eager to press on.

There it was. Funny enough I didn’t get quite as good as some get for the purple, and the colour 
only came out later after I adjusted the lighting, exposure and rather obviously took the eye 
comfort setting off my phone that had filtered out the blue light. But there it was. A purple 
emperor on the ground in front of us. He didn’t stay long and darted back up the track.

Mission accomplished – this was species number 44 – we came across another guy, camera in hand, 
and I asked what he had seen. He had been there over the last couple of days and apparently the 
previous week, there had been far more than today up to 20 I think had been stated. I said we 
had seen “a few”, not realising at the time that one of them was actually a white admiral and 
one was actually the same individual. It was probably only a couple we saw. He pointed us to 
where he had come from where we could try for a few more sightings of the emperors. We parted 
ways and saw his route where the path hit another T-junction, but by this point we were getting 
a bit tired. We could in fact have done a loop all the way around this wood by turning left, 
but we decided to start heading back. That always leaves a bit of a “what might have been” 
feeling, but I was still revelling in that last encounter.

Another chap with gear also crossed our path shortly after, also friendly. We were back in the 
same spot we had photographed the one a few moments ago. He had seen one, possibly the same one, 
and we stood there a while as he indicated he saw it head off into one of the side clearings, 
but could have good back up into the oak canopy. I saw a peacock on the ground. There was no 
sign of the emperor though. There were some nice scarlet pimpernel flowers here too.

We were then back at the car. The plan was then to drive around to the main Fermyn car park,

We then bumped into one of the same fellas that we had seen earlier, now with his partner. 
They were sat down on one of the outside benches and they invited us to join them, where we
