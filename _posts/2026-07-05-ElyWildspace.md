## Essex in Cambs at Ely Common Meadow, Roswell Pits
### Ely Common Meadow, Cambridgeshire

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Ely_Common_Meadow/2026_07_05/Essex_skipper_best.JPG"
       alt="Essex skipper">

After the Fermyn Woods visit, I still had an need to get out and about, and I fancied just a 
simple local stroll down to an area about 15 minutes down the road from me. This was at the 
meadow area as part of Ely Common near Roswell Pits, which is managed for nature and has a 
delightful wildflower meadow. Roswell Pits and Meadows is part of a Site of Special Scientific 
Interest (SSSI) and the meadow area here is looked after by the Ely Wildspace charity. I have 
been here many times before and it always deserves a few visits.

I wandered down and entered via Prickwillow Road.

An initial peacock straightaway greeted me on one of the brambles.

confusion with black-tailed skimmer, but they seem to like hanging around this spot by the entrance.

It was 11:30 by now, and generally it was quite cloudy, but the sun was punching through at times. 
Everything leapt to life across the meadow each time the brightness increased. I continued on my 
route, which headed as if you are going in the direction of the Environment Agency buildings, 
and the main entrance to Roswell Pits. This passes by the main wildflower area. It was a little 
dry and gone over from its heyday earlier in the year but was still providing a fairly decent 
banquet.

Then to my right nearer the main road I had just left, by the brambles, I spotted a moth, a 
latticed heath!

Gatekeepers were highly abundant and as always acting very cool. A painted lady was also nectaring.

Then, after a walk up and down this central spine path at the meadow, as various other walkers 
passed me by, a marbled white appeared!

<figure class="report-figure">

       alt="Marbled white">

I have seen those here before, and even one year had one in my garden. But they do seem to do 
quite well in this meadow.

One other butterfly group that was abundant today were the skippers. I must’ve seen maybe ten 
individuals. I naively assumed at first glance that they would be small skippers, as I do see 
them a lot around the area, in the fields just outside Ely, as well as in my garden too. These 
however, on closer inspection, were Essex skippers, with the straight male line on the forewing 
and diagnostic tips of the antenna ‘dipped in ink’!

In fact, all the skippers I saw here this day were Essex skippers. I hadn’t actually seen such 
a collection since we were at Hampton Court around this time of year.

Not to forget the whites too, as they always provide a backdrop to the show, like this small white.

By this point, I had made my way around the meadow, and had doubled back on a looping route by 
the trees nearer the lake, and I was also half scanning maybe for white-letter hairstreak, 
although I don’t think I saw any Elms here. Wishful thinking, although I had an eye on a trip 
very soon to target them.

More Essex skippers were tucked away as I made my way around, as the path turned towards where 
I entered. This area is quite small so it doesn’t take long for a quick check of what is about.

I scanned along some blackthorn areas nearby, as well as other knapweeds, thistles and other 
flower and grassier patches, just to see what was around. No small copper this time – I have had 
them here before. I headed down towards the sailing club and made my way home. All in all, this 
was a lovely visit, just a stolen hour, and it was exactly the complement I needed to the long 
day before at Fermyn.
