## Euston Drove, Barnham Tue 7 Jul 2026

After the visit to Fermyn Woods, only three days earlier, I had been mulling over other species 
that were on the wing at the moment that I could try and find locally. One species I hadn’t 
yet seen was white-letter hairstreak. Funny enough, there was a good possibility of picking 
this one up very near to where I work in Thetford, Norfolk. There is a small lane that connects 
Barnham just south of Thetford, to an area around the Euston estate further east. A little bird 
had told me that there was a potentially good spot.  White-letter hairstreaks can occur in small 
patches of elm, being so tightly linked that species, and its recovery since Dutch elms disease. 
Any small patches with elms and nectar sources are therefore potentially very interesting spots, 
within their range. This footpath apparently had it all.

This was easily achievable in an extended lunch break, and was only 10 mins drive from my desk. 
I therefore headed down to a convenient layby to pull in to off the busy main road. Simple. Boots 
on, camera at the ready I headed down the path, leading off from the main road.

It was a fine day, sunny and quite hot, although the wind had picked up and was giving a slight 
uncertainty. I stopped at a bramble patch close to the road right at the start of the path, 
nothing there.

A bit further down another patch, lots of flowers already having gone over. However, immediately 
I saw a suspicious small brown butterfly skip from a perch on the brambles. I lost it, but I 
felt that could have been one! I didn’t know for sure but there were lots of gatekeepers, maybe 
more likely?
  
A speckled wood showed well. In fact quite a few species here looked quite fresh, as did a peacock.

I was scanning both tree tops and flower patches all the time. There were some elms here, but 
quite a few other trees such as rows of poplars and oaks. I diverted off the path to a patch 
of bramble, but nothing lept out.

I headed further down the path. Various spots looked good and I figured scanning flowers primarily 
was the best tactic here. Some brambles were still giving lots of flowers and one spot about 
half way down looked really good so I stopped a good few minutes here, but nothing showed. A 
ornet hoverfly was a nice suprise, also called a hornet plumehorn.

I continued on but I was running out of path. It transitioned more to poplar and ferns here and 
I then further reached some buildings, which felt wrong now, and a little too far down. I had 
also lost the elms. I therefore turned back and headed back from whence I came. A dirt bike 
came by me too, and I learned that this is a bike route well-used, having seen some videos later 
on. Cool. A red admiral was hiding amongst the ferns around here too.

I had really liked one of the spots with lots of bramble flowers in full sun and I could see some 
elms, I thought.

I stood there for a few minutes. I had one of those feelings like this is not happening, and 
again I contemplated just calling it a day and tapping out. I was also thinking about the other 
experiences for adonis blue and black hairstreak, and that I was surely unlikely to luck out again.

Then, I noticed something towards the back of the bramble patch. Something small, suspiciously 
hairstreak like, on one of the flowers. Could that actually be one?
  
It was!

Yes! I couldn’t believe it, eye-level not up in the trees, there it was. It was a bit far 
back, cropped heavily but it was clear as day, the white W, and the dark green backdrop was 
stunning. I then lost it as it darted off the flower. But it was likely still here, so 
I stuck around a bit longer.

It then appeared again! And landed close to me on a leaf.

That was amazingly clear.

Its tail was missing and a bit worn, but this one was showing so well. It landed in a plumb spot 
on a bramble right in front of me but would not fully cooperate with the angle towards me and 
again, many shots I ended up discarding, being slightly off my camera game today.

But then it came back down to ground level where I took a few videos too. In fact now thinking 
about it, it was often in quite shady spots on the ground.

This was fantastic. From 13:10 to 13:30 I spent time with this fabulous butterfly, twenty 
minutes basically dancing with it, and an experience that will stick with me. That was species 
number 45. I let it be at this point to go about its business, as did I for that matter needing 
to head back to my desk, already late, although I had a good excuse!
  
I headed back down the path, totally elated. The location I learned was tipped as a good 
spot but it hadn’t been searched for a couple of years. So looking back, this was by no means 
guaranteed, well no butterfly really is. I did have a nagging feeling too that at some point 
my luck would run out with these sightings, but I had put that to the back of my mind for now.

I scampered back to the office and passed a colleague who was interested, another keen lepidoptera 
enthusiast. Apparently, there may be some in Thetford too at a location they had not searched for 
a good long while. Another option, another visit, maybe for tomorrow? Yeah let’s do this.

## Tesco, Green Lane, Thetford, Wed 8 Jul 2026

The next day, the plan was to meet with my colleague to head out to the other spot that may be 
good for white-letter hairstreak. This was a lane near the Tescos in Thetford, a walkway, that 
had apparently been a good spot a few years ago. But it hadn’t been surveyed since then. We headed 
out about 12:20 during a lunchbreak.

It was very hot. It was easily in excess 30 degrees. Sun-creamed up, we headed to the path in 
anticipation. Straightaway we were into elms, all along this stretch. In fact, this was a bit 
like walking back through history as you could clearly see the dead elms from the disease of the 
past, and lots of young elms, saplings and matures trees too. The leaves were distinctive and 
asymmetrical.

Then almost immediately my colleague spotted one. This was classic high up scanning of the canopy, 
proper hairstreak stuff, rather than the comfy eye-height nectaring of yesterday. It was a good 
spot!
  
<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/EustonDrove_GreenLane/GreenLane_view_1.jpg"

We tracked it in the canopy as it landed high up on a leaf. I fumbled in my bag for binoculars, 
my colleague far better prepared at this point, and described its position to me.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/EustonDrove_GreenLane/Leaf_hole.jpg"
       alt="Elm leaves">
  <figcaption>Elm leaves</figcaption>
</figure>

Just leaves were seen for a while. It was near the ‘leaf with the hole in’, a good reference 
point, and then I clapped eyes on it and yes, a white-letter hairstreak for sure! No picture 
though was possible here, as it flitted off to our right too fast, but we had officially seen 
one here. This was good news and another survey point, a dot on the map.

We pressed on down the path. Quite a few holly blues and whites distracted our attention.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/EustonDrove_GreenLane/Holly_blue.JPG"
       alt="Holly blue keeping cool">
  <figcaption>Holly blue keeping cool</figcaption>

We moved up and down the main path, and decided to head back to where we saw the first hairstreak 
and stay put. There were barely any nectar spots around here. That was the big difference to the 
site I visited yesterday. It was hot again in this spot. A good few minutes past with no sign. 
However, it was then my turn to spot something high up; yes, there it was fluttering high in 
the trees, with a characteristic flight pattern.

It moved from where we had seen before, very likely the same one we thought. It moved to some 
larger elms a bit further down beyond our entrance point to the walkway, and in fact in a
slightly better place. There were more elms here. A comma briefly distracted us but then we saw 
the hairstreak perch high up, very high up. Binoculars or camera? My colleague had a bridge camera 
able to digitally zoom. I however still gave a few shots to try and get it.

Well, you can just about see it clear enough to allow an ID so I was quite chuffed with that. 
This opportunity was so much harder than the day before, and made me fully appreciate this 
species’ (and all the hairstreaks for that matter) ellusiveness and reputation for being tricky 
to find. This also put yesterday in even firmer perspective.

We then headed back to the office, high fives all round.
