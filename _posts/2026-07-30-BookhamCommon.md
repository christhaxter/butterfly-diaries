## Another quest for brown hairstreak among the blackthorns of Surrey

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Tiger_moth.JPG"
       alt="Jersey tiger">
</figure>

I had planned a trip to Bookham Common down in Surrey to have a bit of a butterfly jolly. This 
site, managed by the National Trust is a large expanse of oak woodland, Commons and grass plains, 
with lots of blackthorns. Brown hairstreak are well recorded here, so this was well worth a punt. 
I had seen some folks posting on forums that they had seen them.

The week had been good so far with Devil’s Dyke, Aston Rowant and Bernwood Meadows all visited, 
although with no brown hairstreaks found at Bernwood. However, I had a good feeling that Bookham 
Common could deliver one, and hopefully improve upon the Beddington images I had taken.

I aimed to take advantage of a very handy train station right by the side of the Common. It was 
perfect, a green trip, and a railway bridge that then led to a path heading directly into 
blackthorn scrub. I was getting excited when I planned it. A solo mission. An adventure.

I had intended to catch the 7:47 train from Ely originally, but as I was awake early, why not 
go for the 6:47! The early start had an advantage too, in that cloud was predicted to build 
lunchtime onwards, so going slightly earlier would hit a bit more sunshine and open up 
opportunities. Temperatures were going to be quite high up to 28 degrees.

The travel was OK, after a slightly delayed train and busy underground connections. I arrived 
at Bookham at 9:41. Pulling into the station, it was quaint. I could see the exact pathway onto 
the Common. It seemed ideal.

All seemed like it was going well. I then headed through an immediate blackthorn, bramble, 
scrub mix and got onto the Common proper. It was a really good mix of blackthorn and some 
remaining nectar sources.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/View_1.jpg"

I enthusiastically ventured into the Common and started down a less well trodden path that had 
blackthorn and bramble blocking my knees, but with inviting flowers. I turned back when it got 
too thick and picked up a different wider path.

I quickly found myself quite at home on the meandering routes, of which there were many through 
the thicket.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/View_2.jpg"

The usual cast of meadow browns, gatekeepers, small heaths and the odd holly blue were doing their 
thing. And, I do love them all, but boy do they all send you into a: “Is that one? Nope” frenzy 
when searching for hairstreaks.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Holly_blue.JPG"

I then bumped into a couple heading the other direction, one with a camera, and he asked if I 
had seen anything. I mentioned butterflies and brown hairstreaks and he had not had any either, 
but like me they had only just arrived. He was scanning thistle and knapweed heads too. We parted 
ways and I pressed on.

The path here was decent, lots of cattle dung, naturally grazed, and there was so much blackthorn. 
This site is big! I felt a little overwhelmed. Where should I go? I had a rough plan from the maps 
to head into the bit I was currently in, search the blackthorn edges and rides, and make my way 
(if time allowed) over to a known location at Banks Common.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Map.jpg"
       alt="Scrawled map, green star relevant later">
  <figcaption>Scrawled map, green star relevant later</figcaption>

Alas, I didn’t make it that far, instead choosing to spend time around the first blackthorn 
expanse at the Common – see the above yellow route on a poorly printed map; the green asterisk 
will become relevant later.

A family with some beagle dogs also approached from begind me heading my direction. This was nice. 
The lady had said she had seen four other people with cameras and was interested as to what all 
the fuss was about. I explained about the brown hairstreaks, and she regailed me with a tale of 
her finding a purple hairstreak one time. The little girl with her was so enthusiastic and wanted 
to show me a gatekeeper, which was all very lovely. I let them go ahead whilst I scanned yet 
another patch of bramble with binoculars.

Then straightaway another gentleman with a large camera approached. The lady I had just spoken 
to, almost introduced us. We talked butterflies, again hairstreaks, and it turns out he was a 
regular here, perhaps local, I wasn’t sure. He had mentioned where he had hairstreaks in the 
past, specifically mentioning a clearing further up from ahead. This was great! Lots of eyes 
scanning; it felt good at this point. My new friend and I then did a kind of joint search, 
with me following, and we came to the clearing he had mentioned, which had various thistles 
and some bramble and hemp.

He pointed out a jersey tiger moth! Great! Just like the Beddington ones.

  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Tiger_moth.jpg"

I then spotted a small copper on the same patch!
  
<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Small_copper_1.JPG"

We moved on and we had essentially reached the end of the main blackthorn stretch and we were 
near the ‘Isle of Wight’ buildings. I could also hear the lakes nearby too. The path was a track 
now and we were at a T-junction, one way turning right to the wood, which I had no time for today, 
and left looping back to the blackthorns and a car park. The chap then said he was going to double 
back around and headed off and seemed keen to keep scanning the patches he was familiar with.

But, this is where I made the big mistake in the day.

I lost him.

I instead turned on another path back into the thicket, which was still good, but headed back the 
way we had come in parallel to the other path. I was still scannning. A painted lady was nice, 
which just landed on the path in front.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Painted_lady.JPG"
       alt="Painted lady">
  <figcaption>Painted lady</figcaption>
</figure>

However, I was conscious of where he had disappeared to. It may have been in a direction I was 
originally thinking of when I was pouring over the maps, over towards Banks Common.

I needed to return to where we had been so I retraced my steps and moved too quickly through 
the habitat now. I was not scanning closely at this point and my attention slipped. I went back 
to main track and the clearing where we had the small copper – the Jersey tiger was still happily 
nectaring away. I started to move back towards the T-junction, and then he was there.

I asked how he had got on. He had just seen a brown hairstreak! That was amazing. It was only 
about 300 m away and was in a different direction to where I had gone. He showed me his photos! 
I was really pleased for him, albeit slightly envious. However, the fact that there was a good 
chance of success still to be had was very uplifting. He was so kind and offered to show me 
where he had been.

We turned right towards the Isle of Wight buildings; I would never have thought to have gone 
there, and then by the gate of what looks like peoples houses, we veered off to the left on a 
path. There were lots of cows around here, but they were very docile. These were paths well 
trodden by cattle, as they are all over the reserve.

We reached the exact spot he had seen the hairstreak – this is the green asterisk on the map 
above. It was a great spot with many thistles. I said I would stake it out. He wished me luck 
and left me to it, and that was the last I saw of him. The picture below is the precise patch 
of thistles. He even clipped a branch that was in the way for me with some secateurs.

So here is where things went, well, a bit pear-shaped. I did not see anything. I stood for about 
20 mins at first, circled around the spot to other thistles nearby..30 mins…40 mins.

After a while I also decided to also go back and forth to the other clearing spot too where the 
Jersey tiger had been, knowing the chap had said he also had them there in the past. Here, I 
found a consolation prize.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Clouded_yellow_2.JPG"
       alt="A clouded yellow was nectaring!">

Clouded yellow nectaring
For once a clouded yellow was actually stationary for a photo. Well, for a few seconds at 
distance, as shortly after this moment it headed off over the blackthorns. My attention quickly 
shifted back to hairstreaks.

It was 11 am by this point and still quite sunny. I had moved also a bit further back along the 
original path where I had originally entered the site, and finding one of the many small heaths 
dotted around.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Small_heath.JPG"
       alt="A really smart small heath">

It is hard to know what is the best course of action to take in these cases. Should I stick with 
a spot where one has been seen? Should I visit other potential promising areas? I figured, given 
my new friend had just seen it in that specific spot, thatvwas the best move.

I chose to spend most of the remaining time around these exact bushes. However, no hairstreaks 
showed, only browns, and another neat but paler Jersey tiger moth.

Then, the clouds bubbled up. I was losing the sun.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Clouds.jpg"

The butterfly community was buzzing before, but it was much quieter now. Sometimes you just get a 
feel for things changing, and this was one of these moments.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/View_3_clouds.jpg"

I was trying not think negatively, as I knew the conditions would change later on, but I was 
feeling like this was not my day. There were spans of 10 maybe 20 mins now with no sun. At this 
point, I was alternating between spots with thistles. When the sun did come out again briefly, 
I sometimes rushed to get back to the main hairstreak spot if I had moved away from it.

After about another hour scouring the spots, 12 pm now, another small copper got my hopes up. 
They are so smart looking.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Small_copper_2.JPG"
       alt="Another smart-looking small copper">

It got to 12:30, then 13:00 and I was now considering if staying much longer was really worth 
it. I had a bit of lunch at the main spot, whilst trying to keep my eyes fixed on the thistles. 
A ruddy darter was one of many still happily flitting around at this point.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Ruddy_darter.JPG"
       alt="Ruddy darter">

I heading back almost to the start of the common. The cows had decided to follow me.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Cows.jpg"
       alt="Cows keeping me company">

They had actually blocked my through route that I was using to get to and from the hot-spots. 
At one point I decided to squeeze back by the cows as there was a nice bramble patch that kept 
catching my eye. Also several times in the trip, I did have very suspicious butterflies moving 
over the blackthorn tops, moving rather erratic. They could’ve been hairstreaks! They looked 
like the way white-letter hairstreaks fly. Or maybe a frantic small heath, gatekeeper, or holly 
blue playing tricks.

There was also no-one else around now, as if everyone knew something I didn’t.

Another hour had nearly passed and I debated where to get the 14:05 back from Bookham, or to 
give it longer for the 15:05. The sun had come back out again at times. I decided for one last 
dash all the way back to “the spot”, but nothing.

Ok, I was done. I had more or less conceded by now, and had given it my all. This was not to be 
another black hairstreak moment, where it appeared last minute.

Back nearer the original entrance path near the station, another small copper was very cute. It 
posed nicely, as if to say apologetically: “I’m sorry about my mate, for giving you the runaround 
today”.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/BookhamCommon/Small_copper_3.jpg"
       alt="A final small copper sends his apologies">

Also around this point, a really fabulous blue emperor dragonfly cruised by me. Amazing. I didn’t 
get a pic, but what an cool species.

I left the site and caught the 14:05 back to Vauxhall and onwards to Kings Cross and Ely – I ended 
up even getting a slightly earlier train from Kings Cross. At home I reflected on all that had 
happened.

I can’t deny, this trip knocked me a bit. It stung. To have been with the seasoned local who then 
disappeared only to reappear having found a brown hairstreak, to then show me the picture, show 
me the spot, only then for me to still not see it as the clouds rolled in was almost comical. I 
guess this will be a tale to tell, and perhaps we all have such experiences at some point. This 
also came hot on the heels of the other blank we drew at Bernwood. However, although ultimately 
this trip had not delivered the main prize, it had given some brilliant things – the Jersey tiger, 
the clouded yellow, and the small coppers. Also just getting out and about I was thankful to be 
able to do. You can’t be too despondent, as nature always delivers something. The trip had taught 
me a thing or two though, with some valuable lessons in patience, slow steady scanning and staying 
calm. I had set up this trip as a sort of Bernwood redemption to get a better encounter and photo, 
which perhaps added too much pressure. I found it hard to shake those negative feelings in the 
field, which may also have affected my concentration. Also, whenever a local who knows the spots 
is onto something, make sure you stick nearby!
  
Back on a positive note, I had been on a remarkable run of species this year – 18 new species, 
all with a photo that showed ID. Plus I already had seen a brown hairstreak at Beddington. So 
this was always a bonus trip. Brown hairstreaks are notoriously difficult and that’s just the 
way it goes. Middle class problems and all.
