## WWT Welney

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Welney/Small_copper_2.JPG"
       alt="Small copper">
</figure>

I joined some collegues for a day out at Welney, and Lakenheath later, in the fens of East Anglia 
for a nature walk. This was not supposed to be a butterfly outing as such, but a get together to 
enjoy the general wildlife there, of course with a main focus on birds. I wasn’t therefore going 
to include this in the diaries, but enough happened to merit a post.

I arrived at about 8:50 at WWT Welney out in the Ouse Washes, meeting for 9 am. The weather was 
good, in fact quite fortunate given the weather earlier that week was not that great. Well, it 
is mid September after all. Temperatures were forecast above average into the low twenties.

I was a bit early on arrival, however my co-workers arrived promptly and after a quick group 
hello, we headed off through the wonderfully welcoming WWT visitor centre and cafe, casting a 
beady eye on the recent sightings board.

<figure class="report-figure">
  <img src="/butterfly-diaries/assets/images/Welney/Sightings.jpg"

We made our way across the infamous walkway bridge that leads out to the reserve. The reserve 
is centred upon series of bird hides, with the large main observation centre, probably about as 
comfy as bird hides can get. We started here first.

My photos were not quite as good today, heat haze was an issue and for some reason I thought 
it would be a good idea not to add a 1.4 x teleconverter to extend my camera reach. But anyway, 
moan aside, there were some great birds around, even though autumn migration was just getting 
going. We could have used the in built hide scope, but, we had brought our own slightly smaller 
ones 😉.

We were off to a good start. Out of the hide again, butterflies began to show well as the 
sunny skies beckoned them out. A red admiral and a small copper started proceedings, both 
found milling around the ivy flowers.

This was just outside the main observatory hide. A fair few small whites were also around. 
However, away from the hides, alongside the New Bedford River it was pleasant out of any breeze 
and nice and warm. And straight away, the first of the day’s wall browns made an appearance.

Walls were common out here today and actually, that put my Ely sightings into perspective a bit 
more. Ely is only 20 minutes away from the Ouse Washes, but still, urban walls are still a good 
find.

We then had a clouded yellow! There were a few of these around too today, enjoying the grassy 
stretches alongside the river.

We stopped back at the base part of one of the hides, probably the main observatory, I can’t

thought was quite skipper like, but darker. On closer inspection it turned out to be….

A vapourer moth!

## RSPB Lakenheath

After lunch we headed out to Lakenheath. Located not far from Welney, this is more of an inland 
reedbed, meadow, riverine and woodland habitat. They used to have golden orioles here, but not 
since about 2013. Still, there were potentially lots of great things to see here.

We arrived around 15:00, and it was now much cloudier. Butterflies were not on display here, so 
was probably not a fair reflection of the insect population. However, in the car park, a colleague 
and I had spotted a tiger moth fluttering away from us! No time for a picture, but, we think 
likely it was a garden tiger, being too big for jersey tiger.

Into the main visitor centre, it was all very welcoming. And again, a quick check of the 
sightings board is always a good idea.

Common birds were on display such as mallard and moorhen. Our attention was then drawn to a 
small lizard perched on the upcycled wood that formed the edge of the front part of the hide area.

This was a common lizard, and was also joined by a ruddy darter, a bit later. A marsh harrier 
drifted over the distant reedbeds that swayed in the increasing breeze.

Then one of our part skillfully spotted a water rail peek out amongst the moorhens!
  
<figure class="report-figure">

That wasn’t the best shot but at least it was on file! They are normally amazingly secretive 
and usually hard to spot amongst the vegetation. Cettis warblers called and a fair few tits, 
accompanied by warblers, passed in front of our viewpoint over the small waterbody that we had 
now spent about 20 minutes at.

We turned back at this point along the path next to the railway at this point, completing a 
short looping circuit. The rain had sadly set in ao cut our visit a bit short to Lakenheath. 
However, it had still been a decent trip and paired with Welney, it was a great day out of the 
office.
