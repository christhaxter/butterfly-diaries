Motivation

I've been a keen nature enthusiast all my life. My day job is as a bird ecologist, but butterflies have always been another passion. I remember drawing them as a child from identification books and being fascinated by the extraordinary variety of species.

Despite that lifelong interest, I realised that I'd seen fewer than half of the UK's butterfly species. I wanted to get out into the field more, discover some of the less common species and explore the habitats they depend on. This was never just about ticking rare butterflies off a list; it was also about experiencing the wider wildlife of each place and contributing records to conservation and monitoring through iRecord.

A more personal journey

This project has a more personal motivation, too. I lost my father in 2024, turning our family world upside down. Grief is a strange beast, affecting us all in different ways as we learn to live in a world that has changed. It left me conscious of just how short and precious our time here is, and wanting to make the most of it.

At first, I thought that was the main driving force behind this quest. But there was something deeper: a need for distraction, recalibration or reconnection. Another passion of mine is music, and after a year dabbling in songwriting without finding what I was looking for, I turned to wildlife as a refuge.

There is something calming about finding your own rhythm in nature. Getting outside, observing wildlife and immersing myself in a habitat can provide a chance to reset. As a fairly private person, it isn't necessarily easy to put that into words, but it is an important part of why this journey matters to me.

Perhaps some of this resonates with you, too. We all find different ways to get by when life changes, and sometimes the natural world offers those moments of perspective and connection that help us through.

The journey continues

I began 2026 with a life list of around 30 butterfly species and a simple question: how many more could I find, and what could I learn about the places they inhabit along the way? Some species would mean travelling further afield, while others would lead me to discover more of the wildlife closer to home.

This is an ongoing journey, not a challenge to complete in a single season. The species I encounter, the habitats I explore and the plans for future trips will continue to evolve. The aim is to keep learning, keep recording, and keep finding reasons to get out into nature.
