Hockley Woods

WOODLAND · ESSEX

A beautiful ancient woodland in Essex, with oak, hornbeam, sweet chestnut and hazel, interspersed with coppiced clearings and broad woodland rides. Its rich woodland habitat supports a variety of wildlife, and common cow-wheat, the foodplant for the rare heath fritillary.

Aston Rowant

CHALK GRASSLAND · OXFORDSHIRE

Aston Rowant is a beautiful stretch of flower-rich chalk grassland and beech woodland on the Chiltern escarpment in Oxfordshire, offering spectacular views across the Vale of Oxford and excellent opportunities for spotting chalk specialist butterflies, such as the silver-spotted skipper.

Beddington Farmlands

URBAN WETLAND · GREATER LONDON

A restored wetland and nature reserve in south London, with a mix of lakes, reedbeds, grassland and scrub. Its varied habitats attract an impressive range of wildlife, including wintering waterbirds, and with blackthorns aplenty, hosts a population of brown hairstreak butterflies.

Ringstead Downs

CHALK VALLEY · NORFOLK

Ringstead Downs is an ancient glacially-carved valley, and one of Norfolk's last remaining fragments of chalk grassland. Its slopes, flanked by mixed woodland, support a rich variety of wildflowers and invertebrates, making it a rewarding place to explore. Being close to the coast, it can also draw in less common migrant species, such as in 2026 where queen of Spain fritillary was reported.

Ely Common Meadow

TRADITIONAL MEADOW · CAMBRIDGESHIRE

Ely Common Meadow is a traditional area of open grassland and wildflower meadow on the eastern edge of the city, adjacent to the wetlands around Roswell Pits. Its grasslands are managed and maintained as a traditional common and support a variety of wildflowers and butterflies.

Black Fen Way

RIVERINE FENLAND · CAMBRIDGESHIRE

Also called the Black Fen Waterways Trail, the route runs out from Ely and passes through farmland, open fields, light woodland and hedgerows. This route in places passes directly over the black peat of the fens and leads out towards the River Great Ouse and links up with the Fen Rivers Way – the long-distance route between Cambridge and King's Lynn, following the Cam and Great Ouse. It is a rewarding place to look for wildlife, with plenty to explore along the route.

Walton-on-the-Naze

COASTAL GRASSLAND, SCRUB & WETLANDS · ESSEX

Walton-on-the-Naze sits within a diverse coastal landscape of dunes, grassland, scrub, soft cliffs and wetlands, with the extensive tidal creeks and saltmarshes of Hamford Water and the rough grassland, scrub, ponds and grazing habitat of John Weston Nature Reserve nearby. Its position on the Essex coast makes it an important site for wildlife, particularly migrant birds and insects.

East Tilbury

AGRICULTURAL FIELDS & SCRUB · ESSEX

The fields around East Tilbury are perhaps quite unassuming on first appearance, but with a crop left fallow in agricultural fields, together with a large amount of wildflowers, this coastal location near the Thames Estuary was perfectly placed to provide much needed nectar for hungry migrant butterflies arriving on southerly winds in 2026.
