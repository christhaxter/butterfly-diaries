Dingy skipper

Erynnis tages

About the species

The Dingy Skipper is a small, rather inconspicuous butterfly found in scattered locations across Britain. Despite its name, it is a skipper, with a rapid, darting flight and a tendency to settle on bare ground or low vegetation. Its grey-brown wings are marked with darker and paler patches, giving it a mottled appearance that helps it blend into its surroundings.

It is associated with open, sunny habitats where vegetation is generally short and varied, including chalk grassland, woodland rides, quarries and former industrial sites.

Identification

The Dingy Skipper is a small butterfly with grey-brown wings, mottled with darker markings and pale spots. Its understated pattern gives it excellent camouflage against bare soil, stones and dried vegetation.

The underside is similarly mottled, with grey and brown markings. It has a wingspan of around 25–30 mm and a characteristic fast, low flight. Adults frequently bask with their wings open on bare ground or stones.

It can be confused with the Grizzled Skipper, but the Dingy Skipper has a more subdued, mottled pattern rather than the Grizzled Skipper's distinctive white chequerboard markings.

Habitat and distribution

Dingy Skippers occur in a range of open habitats, particularly warm, sheltered grassland with short vegetation and patches of bare ground. Chalk and limestone grassland are important habitats, but the species is not restricted to chalk downland.

It can also be found in woodland rides and clearings, disused quarries, railway embankments and other former industrial sites. These habitats provide the sunny, open conditions and suitable vegetation required by the butterfly.

The caterpillars feed principally on Bird's-foot Trefoil, particularly Common Bird's-foot Trefoil. Adults are often seen basking close to the ground or visiting flowers, and their camouflaged appearance can make them surprisingly difficult to spot.

The species has declined in parts of Britain and occurs in scattered populations. Maintaining open habitat, with a mixture of short vegetation, bare ground and suitable foodplants, is important for its continued survival.
