Grizzled skipper

Pyrgus malvae

About the species

The Grizzled Skipper is a small, lively butterfly found in scattered locations across southern England and Wales. Its dark brown wings are covered in distinctive white markings, giving it a characteristic chequered appearance. Despite its small size, it is an active butterfly, darting rapidly between low vegetation and flowers.

Identification

The Grizzled Skipper has dark brown wings with prominent white spots and markings arranged in a chequered pattern. The underside is also patterned, with pale markings against a brown background.

It is a small butterfly with a wingspan of around 23–29 mm. Its fast, darting flight can make it difficult to follow, but it often settles on bare ground, low vegetation or prominent twigs, sometimes basking with its wings open.

Habitat and distribution

Grizzled Skippers are strongly associated with unimproved chalk grassland, but they also occur in woodland rides, glades and clearings, as well as former industrial sites such as quarries and railway embankments, where short vegetation and patches of bare ground provide suitable conditions.

Adults often bask close to the ground and visit flowers including Bugle and Common Bird's-foot Trefoil. Their rapid flight can make them challenging to follow, but they may settle regularly on prominent twigs or bare ground.

The species has declined significantly in Britain and now survives in scattered colonies. Maintaining open, sunny habitat through appropriate grazing and vegetation management is important for its continued survival.
