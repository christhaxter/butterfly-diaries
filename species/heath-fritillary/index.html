Heath fritillary

Melitaea athalia

About the species

The Heath Fritillary is one of Britain's rarest butterflies, surviving in a small number of locations in southern England. It is a relatively small fritillary, with a distinctive orange and brown chequered pattern across its wings.

It is particularly associated with coppiced woodland, where the butterfly can benefit from the sunny, sheltered conditions created by woodland management. It also occurs in specialised heathland habitat, where suitable foodplants grow among the surrounding vegetation.

Its dependence on carefully maintained habitats makes it a particularly interesting species to encounter. The butterfly has suffered substantial declines in Britain, and conservation work has been important in maintaining its remaining colonies.

Identification

The Heath Fritillary has orange-brown wings marked with dark brown or black lines, forming a characteristic chequered pattern. The underside is similarly patterned, with bands of orange, yellowish and brown markings.

It has a wingspan of around 47–50 mm and is smaller than several of Britain's more familiar fritillaries. Its wing pattern can vary between individuals, with some appearing more strongly marked or darker than others.

Adults fly close to the ground with a characteristic fluttering and gliding movement. They can be seen moving between flowers and low vegetation, often within sheltered areas of their breeding habitat.

The Heath Fritillary can be confused with other fritillaries, particularly the Marsh Fritillary. Its overall pattern, size and habitat can help with identification, although careful examination of the wing markings is useful.

Habitat and distribution

Heath Fritillaries occupy two particularly important types of habitat in Britain. In the south-east, colonies are associated with coppiced or recently felled woodland, especially where Common Cow-wheat grows in the sunny clearings created by woodland management.

In south-west England, the species occurs in sheltered heathland valleys, particularly on Exmoor. Here, it can be found where foodplants such as Ribwort Plantain and Germander Speedwell grow among the heathland vegetation.

The caterpillars feed on a range of plants, including Common Cow-wheat, Ribwort Plantain and Germander Speedwell. The particular foodplants used can vary between colonies, reflecting the different habitats in which the butterfly survives. At Hockley Woods, Essex, for example common cow-wheat is the foodplant founds there.

Adults are generally seen from late spring into summer, with the main flight period falling between May and July. They fly close to the ground and tend to remain within or near their breeding colonies.

The Heath Fritillary has an extremely restricted distribution in Britain, with populations in parts of Kent, Essex and the south-west. Maintaining suitable woodland management and sheltered heathland habitat is essential for its survival.
