Marbled white

Melanargia galathea

About the species

The Marbled White is a distinctive butterfly with striking black and white markings across its wings. Although it belongs to the family of browns and ringlets, its bold pattern makes it stand out from many of Britain's other grassland butterflies.

It is a summer butterfly, typically seen from June into August. Adults can often be found flying over flower-rich grassland, where they visit a variety of flowers for nectar. Their characteristic appearance and lively flight make them a memorable species to encounter on a summer's day.

Identification

The Marbled White has a bold chequered pattern of black and white across both pairs of wings. The upper surface is particularly striking, with dark markings forming a network against the pale background. The underside is also patterned, but with softer shades of grey and white.

Males and females are broadly similar in appearance, although females can be slightly larger and may have a more creamy-white background. The distinctive marbled pattern makes the species relatively straightforward to identify when seen clearly.

Habitat and distribution

In Britain, the Marbled White is mainly found in southern and central England, with its distribution extending into parts of Wales and further north in suitable locations. It favours sunny, flower-rich grassland, including chalk and limestone grassland, roadside verges and other open habitats.

The caterpillars feed on grasses, including species such as Red Fescue and Sheep's Fescue. They develop slowly and overwinter as small caterpillars before continuing their growth in spring. Maintaining open grassland with a good variety of grasses and flowers helps provide the conditions needed by the species.
