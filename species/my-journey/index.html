My Butterfly Journey

This journey was quite ambitious. Call it a healing distraction or a “quest”, but the goal remained the same: to see how many of the UK's butterfly species I could find, while exploring and surveying the habitats they depend on.

That would sometimes mean travelling to distant places to seek out some of our more specialist species. That was fine — it was all part of the fun. The mission, should I choose to accept it, was on. Starting with a life list of 30 species, how much higher could I get in 2026?

Targets in 2026

I developed a rough agenda of target species to try to locate. These were:

Blues — Lycaenidae, subfamily Polyommatinae

  • Small Blue
  • Large Blue
  • Silver-studded Blue
  • Adonis Blue
  • Chalk Hill Blue
  • Northern Brown Argus

Fritillaries — Nymphalidae

  • Marsh Fritillary
  • Heath Fritillary
  • Dark Green Fritillary
  • High Brown Fritillary

Duke of Burgundy — Riodinidae

  • Duke of Burgundy

Browns — Nymphalidae, subfamily Satyrinae

  • Large Heath

Hairstreaks — Lycaenidae, subfamily Theclinae

  • Black Hairstreak
  • Brown Hairstreak
  • White-letter Hairstreak
  • Green Hairstreak

Emperors — Nymphalidae, subfamily Limenitidinae

  • Purple Emperor

Swallowtail — Papilionidae

  • Swallowtail (Papilio machaon britannicus)

Whites and yellows — Pieridae

  • Wood White

Skippers — Hesperiidae

  • Dingy Skipper
  • Grizzled Skipper
  • Silver-spotted Skipper

As well as these targets, I was interested in discovering new habitats and recording all the species I could find within them. In particular, I was interested in rediscovering Silver-washed Fritillary, White Admiral, Clouded Yellow and Purple Hairstreak, even though I had seen these previously.

Green travel

A note on carbon footprints. Living in Ely, Cambridgeshire, placed me within a couple of hours of many of the species on the 2026 agenda. Throughout this challenge, I have been conscious of trying to be a bit greener on my jaunts to more distant places. In particular, I have tried to group trips together, make use of time when we are away anyway visiting relatives, and use train journeys where I can.

However, it is not always perfect, as species tend to inhabit places that can be difficult to access. I do my best to minimise my carbon impact wherever I can.

Thinking ahead: 2027!

This is a multi-year challenge, impossible to achieve in a single year. An update has been added for the species not yet seen and still to locate, along with some thoughts on where to go next.
