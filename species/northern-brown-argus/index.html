Northern brown argus

Aricia artaxerxes

About the species

The Northern Brown Argus is a small butterfly of northern Britain, with a restricted distribution and a preference for open, rocky habitats. It is closely associated with places where its foodplant grows among short vegetation, often on limestone or other calcareous ground.

Although it resembles the Brown Argus, it is a distinct species and has a more limited range in Britain. Its colonies can be localised, making it a special butterfly to encounter when exploring the right habitat.

Identification

The Northern Brown Argus has brown upper wings with a series of orange spots along the margins. These are particularly noticeable on the hindwings, while the overall appearance is darker and less blue than many of the other small butterflies in the blue family.

The underside is grey-brown, with orange markings around the wing edges and dark spots set against a pale background. The pattern can look similar to that of the Brown Argus, so identification is best made by considering the butterfly's location and habitat as well as its markings.

Habitat and distribution

In Britain, the Northern Brown Argus is found in parts of northern England and Scotland. It favours open, sunny habitats with short vegetation, bare ground and rocky patches, particularly on limestone.

Its caterpillars feed on Common Rock-rose, which grows in these warm, open conditions. Maintaining suitable habitat is important for the species, as scrub growth and changes in grazing can reduce the areas of short vegetation it needs.
