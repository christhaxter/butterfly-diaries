Purple Emperor

Apatura iris

About the species

The Purple Emperor is one of Britain's most impressive woodland butterflies, famous for the striking purple sheen of the male's wings. A large, powerful butterfly, it spends much of its time high in the woodland canopy, occasionally descending to feed or investigate the forest floor.

Identification

Purple Emperors are large, dark butterflies with distinctive white bands across the wings and orange-ringed eyespots. Males have an iridescent purple sheen that becomes visible at certain angles in the light, while females are larger and lack the purple colouring.

The butterfly's size, dark colouring and white wing bands make it distinctive, although it can be difficult to observe clearly when flying high among the trees.

Habitat and distribution

In Britain, the Purple Emperor is associated with mature deciduous woodland, particularly areas with sunny rides, clearings and woodland edges. The caterpillars feed on willows, such as goat willow, while adults spend much of their time up in the forest canopy.

The species is widespread in parts of southern and central England, but nonetheless still uncommon and elusive. Established populations exist in suitable woodland. Adults are generally seen during July, when males may descend to investigate minerals, moisture or other food sources on the ground.
