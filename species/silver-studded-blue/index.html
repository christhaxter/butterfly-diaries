Silver-studded blue

Plebejus argus

About the species

The Silver-studded Blue is a small butterfly associated with acidic heathland, where it can sometimes be found in large numbers during the summer. Males have a striking blue upperwing, while females are generally brown with orange markings along the edges of the hindwings.

It is particularly characteristic of lowland heath, where patches of heather, gorse and other low-growing plants provide suitable conditions. The butterfly often flies close to the ground, moving between flowers and basking in sunny openings.

Its caterpillars have an unusual relationship with ants. They produce sugary secretions that attract ants, which in turn tend to protect them from predators. This association is an important part of the species' life cycle.

Identification

The Silver-studded Blue is a small butterfly with a wingspan of around 25–30 mm. Males have bright, inky-blue upperwings with a broad dark border and a fine white fringe.

Females are usually brown above, with orange markings along the hindwing edges and sometimes a variable amount of blue near the body. Both sexes have small tails on the hindwings.

The underside is grey-brown, with black spots surroundedby pale rings and a row of orange markings near the hindwing edge. Small silvery-blue spots near the orange markings give the species its name.

It can be confused with other small blue butterflies, particularly the Common Blue. The Silver-studded Blue is generally smaller, and its underside markings, including the silvery spots, are useful for identification.

Habitat and distribution

Silver-studded Blues are strongly associated with lowland heathland, particularly where heather, gorse and patches of bare ground occur together. They can also inhabit suitable coastal heath and other open, acidic grassland habitats.

The caterpillars feed on a range of low-growing plants, including heathers, gorse and Bird's-foot Trefoil. The foodplants used can vary between colonies, depending on the vegetation available.

The relationship with ants is an important feature of the species' ecology. Caterpillars produce sugary secretions that attract ants, which may provide protection in return. The caterpillars can therefore benefit from the presence of suitable ant colonies as well as their foodplants.

Adults are generally seen from late spring into summer, with the main flight period usually falling between June and August. They often fly low over the heath, visiting flowers or settling on bare ground and low vegetation.

The species has declined in Britain as heathland has been lost, fragmented or allowed to become overgrown. Maintaining open heath, varied vegetation and suitable foodplants is important for its continued survival.
