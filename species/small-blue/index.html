Small blue

Cupido minimus

About the species

The Small Blue is Britain's smallest butterfly, with a wingspan of around 20–25 mm. Despite its tiny size, it is a lively and powerful flyer, often darting rapidly between low vegetation before settling close to the ground.

It is strongly associated with Kidney Vetch, the foodplant of its caterpillars, and is often found in open, sunny grassland where this plant grows. Chalk downland is an important habitat, but the butterfly can also occur in other suitable grasslands, quarries and disturbed ground.

Identification

The Small Blue is a very small butterfly with dark, charcoal-grey or brown upperwings. Males may have a variable dusting of blue scales, sometimes described as 'fairy dust', while females are generally brown and lack this blue colouring.

The underside is grey-brown, with small black spots and a subtle pattern of pale markings. Its tiny size and rapid flight can make it easy to overlook, and it may initially be mistaken for a small Brown Argus.

When settled, the Small Blue often rests with its wings closed, making the patterned underside particularly useful for identification. Males may use prominent low perches within their territories, waiting for females to pass.

Habitat and distribution

Small Blues are found in warm, open habitats where Kidney Vetch grows. They are particularly associated with chalk and limestone grassland, but can also occur on coastal grassland, quarries, railway embankments and other disturbed sites where their foodplant is established.

Kidney Vetch grows well in patches of bare ground and short, sparse vegetation, but can disappear as grassland becomes denser. Small Blue colonies may therefore change location over time, with butterflies moving to new patches of suitable foodplant.

Adults are often seen flying close to the ground, basking on bare soil or low vegetation, and visiting flowers. The species can colonise new areas when suitable patches of Kidney Vetch become available.

Although locally abundant in some areas, the Small Blue has declined in parts of Britain and occurs in scattered colonies. Maintaining open grassland, bare ground and healthy patches of Kidney Vetch is important for its continued survival.
