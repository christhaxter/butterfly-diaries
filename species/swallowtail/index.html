Swallowtail

Papilio machaon

About the species

The Swallowtail is Britain's largest and one of its most spectacular butterflies. Its striking yellow wings, bold black markings and distinctive tails make it unmistakable.

The resident British subspecies, Papilio machaon britannicus, is now restricted to the Norfolk Broads, where it depends on the wetland habitat and its larval foodplant, Milk-parsley.

Identification

The Swallowtail has large, creamy-yellow wings marked with prominent black veins and dark borders. The hindwings have blue markings and distinctive tails, with a red spot near the rear edge.

Its size and unmistakable wing shape make it one of the easiest British butterflies to recognise.

Habitat and distribution

The resident British Swallowtail is associated with the wetlands and reedbeds of the Norfolk Broads, particularly where Milk-parsley (Peucedanum palustre) grows.

The species was formerly more widespread across the East Anglian fens, including parts of Cambridgeshire and Suffolk. Today, the British resident population is confined to Norfolk.

Occasional Swallowtails seen elsewhere in Britain may be migrants or butterflies released from captivity, rather than members of the resident population.
